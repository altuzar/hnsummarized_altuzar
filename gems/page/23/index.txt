12:Tba9,Hengefinder is a web and mobile tool that finds dates when the sun aligns perfectly with street grids, extending the famous Manhattanhenge phenomenon (crowds gathering twice yearly in NYC) to any location globally. Victoria Ritvo built it at the Recurse Center by solving three core subproblems.

Challenge 1: Road Bearing. Computing a street's angle relative to true north requires accounting for Earth's curvature. Naive flat-Earth atan2 on raw lat/lon differences fails because longitude degrees shrink toward the poles while latitude degrees remain constant. The fix: scale longitude by cos(latitude) to put both axes in comparable units before applying atan2. This reflects that east-west motion traces smaller circles at higher latitudes.

Challenge 2: Sun Azimuth and "Sunset". Astral library provides sunset times but defines sunset as when the sun fully dips below the horizon—too late for a henge (sun must be touching horizon). Instead, author targets a specific solar altitude above horizon based on the sun's apparent disk size, then uses binary search to find the last moment the sun remains above that threshold. Rather than seeking an exact value match, this employs a "last true" boundary search pattern, which is less error-prone than traditional binary search formulations.

Challenge 3: Alignment Search. Can't binary search the full year for azimuth=bearing match because solar azimuth is non-monotonic (peaks around solstices). Solution: two-phase search. Phase 1 samples every 30 days, tracking direction changes and applying the Intermediate Value Theorem to flag windows where henges might occur (azimuth crossed bearing or reversed direction). Phase 2 refines those windows day-by-day to find exact alignment dates. Result: one, two, or zero henge dates per year depending on road bearing and latitude.

Website includes an explainer with interactive diagrams. Fellow Recurser John Pribyl extended it to a mobile app adding moon alignments and "Sauron Henges" (sun aligned with building tops, named after Brooklyn Tower).
