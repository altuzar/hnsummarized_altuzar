32:Td52,Cristina Ramos shared a detailed account of engineering a 3D software renderer for Playdate, a handheld with a 168MHz ARM Cortex-M7 CPU and 1-bit monochrome display. She began by writing a raycaster to benchmark floating-point math, memory bandwidth, and framebuffer performance, discovering the device had less power than early 90s 3D accelerators. Despite that, she pursued a visual target resembling 3DO/Sega Saturn games on screen, accounting for the fact that Playdate lacks any graphics hardware.

The technical approach hinged on BSP (binary space partitioning) maps loaded from Quake's format. This let her reuse TrenchBroom level editor and ericw-tools compiler, saving months of work. The BSP tree's precomputed potentially-visible-set (PVS) data eliminates large portions of unreachable geometry each frame, a critical optimization for software rendering. She initially tried painter's algorithm (back-to-front depth sorting) but found a 16-bit reciprocal z-buffer (inverse depth for better precision near camera) offered better ergonomics with masked textures and dynamic objects, with no meaningful performance penalty.

Texture mapping presented a classic trade-off: affine (cheap, warps on non-parallel polygons) versus perspective-correct (mathematically sound but requires per-pixel division).
