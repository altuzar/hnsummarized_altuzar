The Sega CD paired a 7.67MHz Genesis with a 12.5MHz co-processor, adding CD storage but handicapping bandwidth (150 KiB/s single-speed) and access times (800ms). Silpheed appears to use real-time 3D but relies entirely on pre-rendered animation within extreme constraints: 16-color palette, 15fps (sometimes 7.5fps), and only 8 KiB per frame after audio allocation.

The game uses the tilemap rendering model standard on Genesis: images composed of 768 tiles (8x8 each) arranged in a 32x28 grid, then two sprite layers. Rather than storing raw video, Silpheed encodes each frame as tile data plus a tilemap. The compression exploits several properties. First, many tiles are solid colors—Silpheed stores 15 unique single-color tiles and references them repeatedly across the tilemap, achieving 50% bandwidth reduction. Second, the Mega-CD ASIC includes "font registers" meant for fast kanji rendering: a 1-bit bitmap + 2-color palette expands to a full 8x8 tile in hardware.
