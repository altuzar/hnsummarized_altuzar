3f:Td60,LittleLearner is a research project training language models from scratch on pedagogically-filtered text aligned with Common Core K-5 standards. The authors created LittleCurriculum by distilling 88 billion tokens from FineWeb-Edu through a five-stage pipeline explicitly excluding concepts taught above grade 5. They trained three model sizes (0.6B, 1.3B, 5B parameters) on this corpus and paired each with an unfiltered control using identical architecture.

The core research question asks whether interventions like scaling, post-training (GRPO on MathCAMPS), and in-context learning can push models beyond their training data. The answer is starkly no. Scaling improves within-curriculum performance and extends modestly to problems on similar learning trajectories, but yields negligible gains on tasks requiring knowledge outside the K-5 boundary. Post-training amplifies in-scope capabilities but fails to recover beyond-K-5 performance even when explicitly trained on advanced material. In-context learning shows no meaningful improvement on out-of-scope tasks. This suggests that pretraining data distribution sets an effective capability ceiling that standard techniques cannot overcome.

The live chat interface demonstrates typical model behavior. On elementary questions like "what is the five times table," it produces verbose but sometimes confused explanations.
