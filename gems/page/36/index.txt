30:Ta1b,The visualization demonstrates that clothing sizing was never designed to fit most women. Beginning around age 15, girls can wear women's sizes for the first time—and it's the last time the median size matches between cohorts. By her 20s, the median woman wears a size 18, yet most brands cap regular sizes at 16, leaving millions excluded. The problem compounds because: (1) There are no universal standards—each brand invents its own, including different definitions for "Large," "Plus," "Curve," etc. (2) Vanity sizing inflates labels decade by decade (a 1995 size 8 was 2.5 inches smaller in the waist than today's size 8). (3) All sizes derive from a single template—a size 8—graded uniformly like Russian dolls, which distorts proportions as they scale up.
