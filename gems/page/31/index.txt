By layering these stripe patterns at multiple octaves (scales) and blending adjacent cells, the technique creates gullies and ridges that naturally follow slopes. The key innovation solving the "chaotic mess" problem is dividing the pattern into cells with independent pivot points, similar to Worley noise, preventing excessive distortion far from rotation centers.

The original technique had two problems: peaks looked wrong (arbitrary height offsets), and valleys developed unwanted bulges. Johansen presents two solutions. The "frequency approach" (Clay John/Fewes) makes stripe thickness inversely proportional to slope steepness—at peaks/valleys where slope approaches zero, stripes become infinitely thick, always landing on ridge portions of the pattern.
