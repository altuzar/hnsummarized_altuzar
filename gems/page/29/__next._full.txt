27:Tc38,I-DLM solves a fundamental problem with diffusion language models: they traditionally lag behind autoregressive models in quality because they fail at "introspective consistency"—DLMs learn to denoise but not to verify their own outputs. The key innovation is introspective strided decoding (ISD), which generates N tokens per forward pass while simultaneously verifying previously generated tokens using a p/q acceptance criterion based on the base AR model's logit distribution. This unifies generation and verification in one pass, mimicking how AR models train.

The method converts any pretrained AR model (they use Qwen3) via three components: (1) causal attention masking, (2) logit shift for position 1, and (3) an all-masked objective. Training requires only 4.5B tokens on 8 H100s with a stride curriculum (N=2 then N=3). The resulting I-DLM-8B matches Qwen3-8B quality and substantially outperforms all prior DLMs: +26 points on AIME-24 versus LLaDA-2.1-mini (16B), +15 on LiveCodeBench-v6, while using half the parameters. Across 15 benchmarks spanning knowledge, math, code, and instruction-following, I-DLM-8B scores between the base Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B.

Speedup comes from two sources. First, ISD processes 2N-1 queries per forward pass (compared to N queries for stride-based AR), achieving ~2.5x compute efficiency. Second, acceptance rates around 0.9 mean downstream tokens compound geometrically. The throughput-latency trade-off shows I-DLM delivering 2.9-4.1x higher throughput than LLaDA-2.1-mini and SDAR at batch size 64 because it stays memory-bound at higher concurrency levels—each FLOP produces 22% more useful output than AR (efficiency=1.22 vs <0.31 for SDAR). At batch size 1, speedups are minimal due to acceptance rate variance.

A critical advantage: I-DLM uses strict causal attention, enabling direct integration into SGLang with no custom infrastructure. The optional residual ISD (R-ISD) with gated LoRA (rank=128, ~1.12x overhead) guarantees bit-for-bit identical output to the base AR model while maintaining ~2x speedup, offering lossless acceleration. Models and code were released April 12, 2025, with documentation covering training, inference, serving, and benchmarking.

Second, the clock problem: phones have cheap quartz clocks that drift microseconds, causing meters of position error.
