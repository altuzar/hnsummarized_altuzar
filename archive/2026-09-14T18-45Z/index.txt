The name is historical: before ud2 existed, programmers discovered that 0F FF and 0F B9 byte sequences reliably triggered invalid opcode exceptions. Over time, both patterns accumulated users relying on this behavior.

Intel encountered Hyrum's Law when designing new processors. Changes that tried to repurpose 0F FF or 0F B9 broke existing software that had come to depend on them raising exceptions. Rather than fight this, Intel decided to formalize an undefined instruction.
