TLA+ divides systems into behaviors (state sequences) and checks properties using temporal logic: []P (always), P' (next state), <>P (eventually). Safety properties like invariants ([]P) and action properties ([](x' >= x)) prevent bad states. Liveness properties (based on <>) ensure good things happen. Most checks are invariants or liveness compositions like []<>P or [](P => <>Q).

TLA+ cannot express: properties lacking logical formalization, multi-step properties ("delete then undo returns original"), floating-point or real-time properties, reachability ("a winning move exists"), hyperproperties ("energy mode uses less power than normal across all identical action sequences"), or state-space metaproperties ("only one path from X to Y"). The core limitation is that TLA+ properties are universally quantified over behaviors—true in every behavior—while reachability and hyperproperties require existential or multi-behavior quantification.

Workarounds exist but are inelegant: auxiliary variables (break refinements), self-composition (exponential state-space explosion), or switching tools (CTL for reachability, PRISM for probabilistic properties), each with tradeoffs.
