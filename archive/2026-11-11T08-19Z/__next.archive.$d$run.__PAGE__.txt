The core insight replaces Lloyd's full-batch updates with mini-batch exponential moving average (EMA) updates: S_j ← (1-α)S_j + αs_j instead of S_j ← s_j. This allows the centroid to track recent assignments rather than the full history, making the optimization landscape noisier and better at escaping local minima. Combined with cosine-scheduled learning rate decay, improved initialization, dead-cluster revival via proportional splitting (not random resampling), and finalization stage, e-means needs only 1.2-3 epochs to reach Lloyd's loss at 25 epochs.

The paper validates extensively across 24 datasets with 100k-100M points. Wall-clock speedups: 10-45x vs Flash-KMeans on GPU (H100), 8-15x vs Faiss on CPU. Toy visualizations show e-means escapes pathological configurations (Lloyd stuck 15-46% of starts, e-means 0-3.5%). A key contribution is automatic hyperparameter selection: scaling laws fit to problem dimensions (n, k, T) automatically set batch size B and initial step α₀ without tuning, generalizing well to held-out datasets (R² ≈ 0.95). Dead-cluster revival is critical for large codebooks—proportional splitting reduced dead clusters from 2,343 (EnCodec resampling) to 47 on DEEP dataset (1M vectors, k=16,384).
