The real threat is negative leap seconds: Earth's rotation is slowing, but computations suggest a negative leap second (removing a second) might be needed by 2035, something no production system has ever handled and few have tested. The workshop data cited indicates 30% probability by 2035. Industries including IoT, critical infrastructure (power grids, telecom, transportation), and satellite systems depend on continuous time without jumps. Global providers already inconsistently mitigate leap second insertion by hiding or absorbing the step, violating SI conformance. The emerging risk that some organizations will abandon UTC for GNSS time scales threatened the concept of international standardization.

The resolution allows UT1-UTC to drift up to ±3600 seconds (one hour) before mandatory adjustment, pushing the problem far enough into the future that leap-hour handling becomes trivial—many countries already do hour-long shifts twice yearly via daylight saving time.
