Hyndman and Bisin's failed replication prompted investigators to examine the original data files, which had been shared with Hyndman in 2006 from dan.ariely@mit.edu. Four major red flags emerged:

Red Flag 1: Effect size implausibly large. The performance difference between conditions yielded Cohen's d=2.5, with evenly-spaced participants making 136 corrections versus 71 for last-deadline participants. This exceeds gender differences in height (d≈1.8) and other well-established psychological effects. Red Flag 2: Duplicate observations. Eighteen of twenty last-deadline participants had "correction twins"—another participant with identical error counts on all three separate tasks. These twins had ID numbers exactly 10 apart (S1/S11, S7/S17, etc.). No twins appeared in other conditions. The probability this occurs by chance is negligible.

Red Flag 3: Missing correlations. Subjective measures that should correlate highly don't. Original data: liking-interest correlation near zero. Replication data: r near perfect.
