The core insight: 91% of tokens spent by agents go to reading files, running tools, and exploration. Only 9% go to actual edits and writing. When you hand a cheap model a "plan," it must re-read all the files the frontier model already read—paying the cheap model's full token price for context that was already cached on the frontier model. Their solution, "/prewalk," prefills the cheap model's context with the frontier model's exploration history and a structured todo list. The frontier model does the research and makes one confident edit, then the execution swaps to the cheap model but keeps the context window. The cheap model continues with that context already loaded in its KV cache, avoiding re-reading. Results: $1.46 (47% cheaper than Opus alone), 78% pass rate (92% of Opus performance), and 34% faster due to offloading slow frontier tokens.

The technique rests on an older principle: autoregressive models don't distinguish between tokens they generated and prefilled tokens fed to them. By prefilling "here's the exploration, here's the plan, now execute," the model continues naturally without feeling instructed.
