Using photoelectron spectroscopy on bismuth-carbon molecules cooled near absolute zero, they demonstrated that the textbook description of triple bonds—one sigma bond plus two pi bonds—breaks down when atomic nuclei are heavy enough that electrons approach significant fractions of light speed.

In heavy elements, spin-orbit coupling becomes important: electron spin and orbital motion are no longer independent, causing the classical distinction between sigma (head-on) and pi (side-by-side) bonds to blur. Instead of the traditional structure, bismuth-carbon bonds showed a pattern consistent with one pi bond and two hybrid sigma-pi bonds.
